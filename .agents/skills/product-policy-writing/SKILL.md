# Product policy writing

Create or review formal product policies from current product behavior and
approved legal positions. This skill organizes the work. The reference holds
the detailed, sample-derived writing guidance. This skill does not provide
legal advice or approve policy text.

## Process

1. Define the request.

   Identify the policy type, product or service, audience, regions, user groups,
   effective-date intent, related policies, and whether the request is to
   draft, revise, or review. Ask one focused question when a missing scope
   decision would materially change the document.

   Completion criterion: the deliverable has a documented scope and an explicit
   policy type, or the unresolved choice is visible.

2. Ground the work in evidence.

   Inspect the supplied product materials and the active provider materials
   relevant to the request. Keep a claim ledger that distinguishes verified
   product facts, reader obligations or choices, unverified facts, and legal
   approval items. Attach each material claim to its source or approval path.

   Treat planned behavior and assumptions as unresolved. If a material fact is
   missing, ask for it or mark it for confirmation. Do not fill the gap with
   invented product behavior or legal substance.

   Completion criterion: every material claim needed for the deliverable has a
   source, a confirmation owner, or a legal approval path.

3. Route to the reference.

   Read the relevant sections of [the policy-writing reference](references/policy-writing-guidelines.md)
   before drafting or reviewing. For every supported policy, apply its
   [source-of-truth rule](references/policy-writing-guidelines.md),
   [sample-derived style](references/policy-writing-guidelines.md),
   [document anatomy](references/policy-writing-guidelines.md),
   [voice and sentence patterns](references/policy-writing-guidelines.md),
   and [definitions and cross-document language](references/policy-writing-guidelines.md)
   guidance to every supported policy.

   Read [provider and dependency research](references/policy-writing-guidelines.md)
   when provider or integration claims are material. Read the [policy-set map](references/policy-writing-guidelines.md)
   when deciding which documents or topics belong in a policy set. Then read
   the relevant policy-specific section:

   - [Privacy policy](references/policy-writing-guidelines.md)
   - [Cookie policy](references/policy-writing-guidelines.md)
   - [Terms of use](references/policy-writing-guidelines.md)
   - [Usage policy](references/policy-writing-guidelines.md)
   - [Privacy governance](references/policy-writing-guidelines.md)

   For a policy-set review or publication preparation, also read the [cross-policy consistency review](references/policy-writing-guidelines.md)
   and [publication checklist](references/policy-writing-guidelines.md).
   The reference is sample-derived guidance, not reusable legal substance. For
   an uncovered policy type, say that the reference does not cover it.

   Completion criterion: every planned section is covered by the applicable
   reference guidance and has a source or approval path.

4. Produce the requested work.

   Draft only supported product behavior and approved legal positions. Keep
   unresolved facts and legal decisions separate from operative copy. During a
   review, report the location, issue, source or missing evidence, user impact,
   and recommended resolution. Do not silently choose an unapproved legal
   position or remove a material condition or exception.

   Completion criterion: the draft or review communicates supported flows,
   responsibilities, controls, and consequences without invented substance.

5. Finish with consistency checks.

   Run the applicable consistency and publication checks from the reference.
   List unresolved items explicitly. Do not imply that a policy is ready for
   publication until product and qualified legal reviewers have approved it.

   Completion criterion: every applicable check is complete or has a named
   unresolved item.

## Return format

Return:

1. The requested policy draft or review findings.
2. A concise fact and source summary.
3. Unresolved product, provider, and legal-approval items.
4. Cross-policy inconsistencies and links or controls that need verification.
5. A status such as "Draft for product and legal review."
